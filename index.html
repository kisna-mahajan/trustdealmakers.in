Redeploy Globally

Old Does Not Mean Worthless

Industrial equipment does not necessarily lose its value because a company has replaced it. A machine may have become surplus because a plant was modernised, a production line no longer fits the owner’s strategy, or a complete plant was shut down, relocated or replaced. A CNC machine may be technologically old for one manufacturer but entirely suitable for another. A rolling mill, furnace, process plant or specialised production line may still have years of productive life left in it.

Redeploy Globally helps owners of surplus industrial assets find that next productive home — connecting them with international buyers, investors, manufacturers and operators seeking cost-effective production capability.

The Opportunity, in One Line

Surplus Asset → Identify Market → Find Buyer → Qualify Interest → Develop Transaction. The objective is not simply to dispose of machinery — it is to discover its next productive commercial life.

The Opportunity

Your Surplus May Be Someone Else’s Production Opportunity

Industrial companies regularly upgrade their manufacturing capabilities — installing newer CNC machines, robotics, furnaces, rolling mills and production lines. As a result, perfectly usable equipment becomes surplus to the present owner. At the same time, manufacturers elsewhere are looking for exactly that.

What Gets Replaced

  • CNC machines & robotics
  • Furnaces & rolling mills
  • Production & process lines
  • Material-handling systems
  • Electrical & control systems

What Other Markets Are Seeking

  • Proven machinery, complete lines
  • Cost-effective manufacturing capacity
  • Technology embedded in existing equipment
  • Complete plants, spare capacity
  • Replacement & second-hand equipment

What is surplus to one organisation may be productive capital for another. This is a genuine cross-border commercial opportunity — and age alone does not determine commercial value. A machine may be old but well maintained, operational, mechanically sound and economically attractive compared with new equipment. Application, condition, configuration and market demand matter far more than the year on the nameplate.

Industrial modernisation across India and elsewhere continues to create this opportunity. As companies replace older production lines, legacy automation and complete plants, the resulting surplus becomes exactly what manufacturers in markets with growing industrial demand and lower capital availability are looking for.

Our Approach

We Do Not Start with “How Much Is It Worth?”

We start with a more important question: “Who could actually use it?” The commercial value of an industrial asset depends on far more than its original purchase price — and a machine with limited value in one market can have meaningful value in another.

Don’t Just Send Us a Photograph

A photograph is useful. A technical specification is much more useful. The better the information, the more intelligently the opportunity can be presented to potential buyers.

Asset & Technical Information

  • Manufacturer, make, model, year
  • Capacity, dimensions, power
  • Control system & automation level
  • Location & operating status

Condition

  • Operational / non-operational
  • Maintenance history
  • Last operating date
  • Availability of spare parts

Commercial Information

  • Owner’s expectations
  • Individual machine or complete line
  • Inspection & dismantling support
  • Expected transaction structure

Complete plant or individual machine? Both may have a market. Sometimes the most attractive proposition is a complete plant — an international buyer may want the integrated manufacturing capability, not just individual machines. In other cases, a single CNC machine to a specialist manufacturer, a furnace to a steel producer, or a production line to a manufacturer in an emerging market is the more commercially attractive route. If you have an entire plant, don’t dismantle it before understanding the market — the strategy depends on the asset and the buyer ecosystem.
