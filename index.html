Redeploy Globally

Old Does Not Mean Worthless

Industrial equipment does not necessarily lose its value because a company has replaced it. A machine may have become surplus because a plant was modernised, a production line no longer fits the owner’s strategy, or a complete plant was shut down, relocated or replaced. A CNC machine may be technologically old for one manufacturer but entirely suitable for another. The Opportunity

Your Surplus May Be Someone Else’s Production Opportunity

Industrial companies regularly upgrade their manufacturing capabilities - installing newer CNC machines, robotics, furnaces, rolling mills and production lines. As a result, perfectly usable equipment becomes surplus to the present owner. At the same time, manufacturers elsewhere are looking for exactly that.

What is surplus to one organisation may be productive capital for another. This is a genuine cross-border commercial opportunity - and age alone does not determine commercial value. A machine may be old but well maintained, operational, mechanically sound, supported by available spares, proven in production, suitable for a specific application and economically attractive compared with new equipment. Conversely, a relatively new machine may have limited buyer interest if its specifications are highly specialised or obsolete. We do not automatically classify equipment as valuable or worthless based only on age - application, condition, configuration and market demand matter far more than the year on the nameplate.

Industrial modernisation across India and elsewhere continues to create this opportunity. As companies modernise their manufacturing systems, they may replace older production lines, conventional machine tools, manual equipment, legacy automation, older furnaces, process systems and complete plants. The resulting surplus becomes exactly what manufacturers elsewhere are looking for - particularly where the receiving market has lower capital availability, growing industrial demand, existing technical capability, demand for proven equipment and a need for economical production capacity.

Our Approach

We Do Not Start with “How Much Is It Worth?”

We start with a more important question: “Who could actually use it?” The commercial value of an industrial asset depends on far more than its original purchase price - and a machine with limited value in one market can have meaningful value in another.

Commercial value can depend on age, make, model, technical specification, capacity, condition, maintenance history, automation level, production application, availability of spares, documentation, installation requirements, dismantling requirements, transport economics, buyer demand and destination-market requirements. Because a machine with limited value in one market can have meaningful value in another, market identification and buyer identification are central to the redeployment process.

3. What are the four commercial pathways?

Bring to India (India market entry for international companies), Take to the World (global market development for Indian companies), Redeploy Globally (international buyers for industrial assets), and Strategic Partnerships (technology, OEM, manufacturing and commercial alliances).

Bring to India helps international manufacturers, technology companies and industrial organisations develop customers, partners and commercial presence in India. This includes market entry, customer development, commercial representation, distribution, OEM partnerships and strategic sourcing.

5. What is Take to the World?

Take to the World helps selected Indian manufacturers and technology companies develop international markets, identify overseas buyers, distributors, OEM customers and build global commercial relationships.

6. What is Redeploy Globally?

Vision 2036

India's Next Decade: From Manufacturing Capability to Global Industrial Leadership

Trust DealMakers' long-term commercial perspective on India's industrialisation, manufacturing, technology, global supply chains and international commercial opportunities.

India is entering a period in which the opportunity is larger than simply becoming a bigger domestic market. The next decade can position India as a major manufacturing base, a technology-adoption market, a global sourcing destination, an engineering capability centre and an increasingly important node in global supply chains.

This is not a government policy document or investment forecast. It is a commercial perspective based on the intersection of industrial technology, manufacturing, international business, market access and cross-border relationships.

The Commercial Opportunity

Three Major Commercial Flows

We see three commercial flows becoming increasingly important between now and 2036.

01

Technology Into India

International technology → Indian market → Indian manufacturing → localisation → scale. India cannot become a global industrial powerhouse without technology partnerships.

02

Indian Products Into the World

Indian manufacturing → international market → overseas buyer → repeat business → global supply chain. Indian companies can move from exporters to global strategic suppliers.

03

Industrial Capability Across Borders

Technology + manufacturing + capital + market access + strategic relationships. The deeper India-global commercial ecosystem connecting complementary capabilities.

India's Industrial Opportunity

From low-cost manufacturing to high-value industrial capability

India's opportunity should not be defined only by labour cost. The stronger proposition is a combination of engineering, manufacturing, technology, talent, market and scale.

Advanced Manufacturing Machine Tools Industrial Automation Robotics Electronics Semiconductor Ecosystems Steel & Heavy Engineering Aerospace Defence Manufacturing Healthcare Technologies Industrial Chemicals Renewable Energy Engineering Services Global Sourcing OEM Manufacturing

What This Means

The Bridge Between Capability and Opportunity

For International Companies

By 2036, the question may not simply be "Should we enter India?" - it may be "How important should India become to our global strategy?" India could serve simultaneously as market, manufacturing base, sourcing base, technology market, engineering base and supply-chain partner.

For Indian Companies

The question may become "Can we become part of a global company's supply chain?" or "Can our technology compete internationally?" or "Can we build an international business rather than simply make occasional exports?"

For Technology Companies

Technology companies entering India may need more than a distributor. They may need market intelligence, customer access, manufacturing partners, OEM relationships and strategic alliances. The opportunity is two-way.

For Industrial Asset Owners

Industrial modernisation creates surplus - but surplus does not mean obsolete. The international market can provide a productive second life for machinery and plants that are still commercially viable.

Our 2036 Principle

Build Relationships That Create Industrial Value

2036 will not be created in 2036. It will be created through the technology decisions, manufacturing investments, international relationships, partnerships and commercial initiatives developed during the decade before it.

For both international and Indian companies, the critical requirement is often the same: the right technology, the right market, the right company, the right people, the right relationship. Trust DealMakers exists to help bring those elements together.
